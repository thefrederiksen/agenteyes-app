WHY THIS IS NOT TEN APP RECORDINGS, said plainly. The app cannot be made to capture this microphone at 48 kHz:
dshow is given no -sample_rate and picks 44.1 kHz on this Realtek mic. Changing the Windows "Default Format" does
NOT change that - measured: the endpoint's shared-mode format is ALREADY 2 ch / 16 bit / 48000 Hz (read through
IPolicyConfig::GetDeviceFormat, nothing written), and dshow still delivers 44100 Hz. So the closest real 48 kHz
case is:
  1. CAPTURE: the app's exact capture command (copied from a C1 manifest's FfmpegCommand: gdigrab 1920x1080 30 fps
     + dshow, thread_queue_size 1024, audio_buffer_size 80, libx264 veryfast crf 23, aac 128k) with ONE addition,
     "-sample_rate 48000" on the dshow input, and a -t length. The mic accepts 48000 (it is in its dshow mode list).
     Lengths chosen so every capture ends PART-WAY through a 480-sample frame - four first attempts happened to land
     on whole frames (AAC's 1024-sample frames align with 480 every 15 frames) and were re-captured, because a
     whole-frame capture would not exercise the end-of-stream condition at all.
  2. POST-PROCESS: the product's own call, AudioMix.ProcessVideoMic(raw.mp4, recording.mp4, options) - the call the
     app's mux stage makes for a mic-only video recording - one folder per capture, with the app's mic-only
     defaults (denoise on, leveling on, gate off: the chain the app logged for the C1 runs). Run under the
     product's stall watchdog. The harness was a temporary test file, not committed.
